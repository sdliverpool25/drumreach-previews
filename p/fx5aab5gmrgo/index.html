The diagnostic

5 places Cygnum could be bleeding time and money

Answer honestly and tick the box if it applies. The figures are illustrative estimates based on stated assumptions — not your actual numbers, which we don't have.

AREA 01

Take-offs counted by hand

How long does it take to go from architect's drawings to a priced quote?

LOOK FOR
  • Panel, opening and length quantities counted manually
  • Re-measuring when drawings change
  • Estimating backlog in busy periods
  • Variance between estimate and as-built
Estimated bleed (illustrative): If 3 staff each spend 10 hrs/week on this at an assumed EUR 40/hr: 3 × 10 × EUR 40 × 4.33 weeks ≈ EUR 5,200/month. Illustrative assumption, not your figures.
AREA 02

Quotes waiting in a queue

When volume nearly doubles, does quote turnaround hold — or do builders wait?

LOOK FOR
  • Quote turnaround not measured
  • Senior estimators as bottleneck
  • Lost jobs not linked to slow quotes
  • Same clarifications asked each job
Estimated bleed (illustrative): If faster quoting won just 1 extra house-package a month at an assumed EUR 4,000 margin, that's ≈ EUR 4,000/month (illustrative assumption).
AREA 03

Production schedule juggled manually

How is the factory schedule rebuilt when a site slips or a drawing changes?

LOOK FOR
  • Schedules in spreadsheets or whiteboards
  • Changeovers not optimised
  • Late design changes rippling through
  • Capacity for the new line planned by feel
Estimated bleed (illustrative): If 2 staff each spend 5 hrs/week on this at an assumed EUR 40/hr: 2 × 5 × EUR 40 × 4.33 weeks ≈ EUR 1,730/month. Illustrative assumption, not your figures.
AREA 04

Shop-floor data on paper

Do you know, per panel, how long it took and where it waited?

LOOK FOR
  • Paper job sheets
  • Time-per-panel not captured
  • Rework not logged against cause
  • QC records hard to search
Estimated bleed (illustrative): If 8 staff each spend 1 hrs/week on this at an assumed EUR 30/hr: 8 × 1 × EUR 30 × 4.33 weeks ≈ EUR 1,040/month. Illustrative assumption, not your figures.
AREA 05

Scaling the team faster than the process

With 150 jobs coming, how quickly does a new hire become productive?

LOOK FOR
  • Know-how held by long-serving staff
  • Procedures not written down
  • Training done shoulder-to-shoulder
  • Group reporting adding admin
Estimated bleed (illustrative): Qualitative — slower ramp-up for new staff and senior time spent training. No estimate.
