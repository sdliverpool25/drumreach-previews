Positioning and USP1

One-line positioning. AIworkshere.ie is a free weekly email for managers and owners of firms in Cork, Kerry and Limerick. Each issue gives one practical AI idea that works here: on the Microsoft 365 and SAP/ERP systems you already own, under Irish and EU rules, with the local funding, supports and events that help.

Tagline: "Practical AI that works here."

USP: what makes it different

  1. Local and specific. It's written from Killarney for Munster firms and covers local events, LEO and Enterprise Ireland supports, and local examples. None of the AI newsletters we reviewed focuses on Cork, Kerry and Limerick (see section 4).
  2. Built around the stack you already own. Every idea maps to Microsoft 365, Copilot, Power Platform, Power BI or SAP (Joule). It never starts with "buy a new tool".
  3. Written by a builder, not a commentator. Shane builds rapid prototypes, pilots and Power Apps. Issues show how the work gets done (the steps, the data, the risks).
  4. Time back, measured in hours. Every issue ends with a "time-back test": how to estimate the staff hours a week the idea could free up in your firm.
  5. Funding and rules in plain English. Each issue maps ideas to a real support (for example the LEO Grow Digital Voucher, EI Digital Discovery or ENTIRE EDIH) and to the EU AI Act, with links to official sources.

How it relates to the brands. AIworkshere.ie is the authority and top-of-funnel channel for Process Power ("operational AI that gives your team time back"). Calls to action lead to Process Power offers: a readiness assessment and roadmap, rapid prototypes, pilots and Power Apps. DrumReach (GBP/SEO) stays separate and is not promoted in the newsletter.

Target audience2

The audience has two primary groups, as Shane decided, plus multipliers.

Primary group A: managers at Munster firms on Microsoft 365 or SAP

A1. "Operations Olivia": operations, finance or managing director

  • Works at a manufacturer, food or agri processor, distributor or engineering firm in Cork, Kerry or Limerick (roughly 50 to 1,000 staff), running Microsoft 365 plus SAP or another ERP.
  • Owns the budget and the problem: overtime, slow month-end, quote backlogs, reporting.
  • Wants proof that AI saves hours, at low risk, without hiring.
  • Path to client: readiness assessment or pilot, then a €3k to €5k a month retainer.

A2. "IT Ian": IT manager or head of IT in the same firms

  • Gets asked about Copilot rollout, governance, data access and "shadow AI".
  • Wants clear Microsoft updates, security and compliance guidance, and rollout steps.
  • Path to client: an internal champion who brings Process Power in for a prototype.

Primary group B: SME owners

B1. "Owner-MD Maura": owner or managing director of an SME (roughly 20 to 250 staff) in professional services, engineering, hospitality and tourism groups, distribution or retail. Usually already pays for Microsoft 365.

  • Keeps hearing about Copilot and ChatGPT and wants to know where AI saves time, what it costs, which funding helps and what the rules are.
  • Time-poor, so she needs a short, plain read.
  • Path to client: owners of firms with about 30 or more staff, or high margins, can buy a roadmap and a retainer. Smaller owners are valuable as readers and referrers, and are a fit for smaller pilots.

Fit check: most firms with 10 to 49 staff won't pay €3k to €5k a month. Write so owners of every size get value, but aim booking calls to action at firms of about 30+ staff.

Multipliers (not buyers, but they spread the word)

  • Ecosystem: LEO business advisers, chamber staff, ENTIRE EDIH and RDI Hub teams, Enterprise Ireland development advisers, meetup organisers. They forward content and can offer speaking slots (Authority KR3).
  • Partners: accountants, Microsoft partners and MSPs.
  • Local builders: Power Platform and full-stack developers (Cork Devs, Future of Data Cork). They are potential subcontractors and referrers.

Value proposition (canvas-style)3

Customer jobs

  • Cut admin and rework: retyping and reconciling data across ERP, Excel and Microsoft 365.
  • Produce quotes, tenders, spec sheets, compliance paperwork and month-end reports faster.
  • Decide whether Copilot or other AI is worth paying for, and roll it out safely.
  • Stay compliant (EU AI Act, GDPR) without becoming a lawyer.
  • Find out what grants, supports and training exist locally.

Pains

  • Too much hype and vendor noise, and too little that applies to a Munster firm.
  • Not sure what existing Microsoft licences already include.
  • Worried about data leaks and staff using public AI tools without guidance.
  • No in-house data or AI person, and past IT projects that ran long.
  • No time to track AI news, rules and events.

Gains

  • One five-minute read a week with one idea to try.
  • Worked examples on familiar tools (Excel, Teams, SharePoint, Power BI, SAP exports).
  • Plain-English summaries of rules and funding, with links to official sources.
  • A local calendar of AI events and supports.
  • A rough hours-saved estimate to take to the board, the MD or the finance director.
  • A trusted local person to ask when you're ready to run a pilot.

What the newsletter does to deliver this

  • Filters the news down to 2–3 links that matter, each with a "so what for you" line.
  • Runs a "Microsoft and SAP corner" linked to official release notes.
  • Includes a "Try this" box with one safe prompt or workflow, and a "Money and rules" box.
  • Offers lead magnets: an AI readiness checklist (System KR3 / SHA-31) and a one-page safe-AI guide (System KR5 / SHA-33).
  • Answers readers' replies in a later issue ("Ask Shane").

Content pillars and format6

Pillars (rotate weekly)

  1. Time back (use cases): one operational AI workflow on M365 or ERP data, such as reconciliation, quotes, reports or document handling.
  2. Microsoft and SAP: Copilot, Power Platform, Power BI and SAP Joule changes that matter, linked to official release notes.
  3. Safe and compliant: EU AI Act, GDPR, AI policies and staff guidance, linked to official sources.
  4. Local supports and community: LEO, Enterprise Ireland, ENTIRE EDIH, the RDI Hub, research centres, and events in Cork, Kerry and Limerick.
  5. Plan and lead: roadmaps, business cases and "how to brief your board".

Fixed format (about a five-minute read, 500–700 words, Tuesday mornings around 8am; test the send time)

  1. The big idea (300–500 words): one problem, one approach, the steps.
  2. Time-back test: how to estimate the hours saved in your own firm.
  3. This week in Irish AI: 2–3 curated links, each with one line on why it matters.
  4. Microsoft and SAP corner: one relevant update.
  5. Money and rules: one funding support or one AI Act point.
  6. Try this: one safe prompt or workflow.
  7. Munster AI diary: upcoming events and supports.
  8. Ask Shane, plus one CTA: answer a reader question, then one call to action (reply, share, lead magnet or book a call).

Repurposing: each issue becomes one LinkedIn post, one digital-clone video script (Authority KR2) and one carousel. The web archive lives on aiworkshere.ie (Beehiiv site) for SEO.

Rules: no invented stats or quotes. Anonymise client examples or label them as illustrative. Link to the primary source. Get written permission before naming any person or company.
