Positioning and USP1

One-line positioning. AIworkshere.ie is a free weekly email for people who run operations in Cork, Kerry and Limerick firms. Each issue covers one practical AI idea that works here: on the Microsoft 365 and ERP systems you already own, under Irish and EU rules, with local supports and events.

USP: what makes it different

  1. Local and specific. It's written from Killarney for Munster firms, with local events, LEO and Enterprise Ireland supports, and local examples. It is not a global AI hype digest.
  2. Built around the stack you already own. Every idea maps to Microsoft 365, Copilot, Power Platform, Power BI or SAP/IFS. It doesn't start with "buy a new tool".
  3. Written by a builder, not a commentator. Shane builds rapid prototypes and works as a forward-deployed engineer. Issues show how the work actually gets done (the steps, the data, the risks) rather than opinion.
  4. Time back, measured in hours. Every issue ends with a "time-back test": roughly how many staff hours a week the idea could free up, and how to check that in your own firm.

Tagline options: "Practical AI that works here." / "AI that works here, in your systems, with your team, in your county."

How it relates to the brands. AIworkshere.ie is the authority and top-of-funnel channel for Process Power ("operational AI that gives your team time back"). CTAs lead to Process Power offers (readiness assessment and roadmap, pilots, prototypes). DrumReach (GBP/SEO) stays separate and is not promoted in the newsletter, so the message stays focused.

Value proposition (canvas-style)2

Customer jobs

  • Cut admin and rework: retyping and reconciling data across ERP, Excel and Microsoft 365.
  • Produce quotes, tenders, spec sheets, compliance paperwork and month-end reports faster.
  • Make decisions on current numbers rather than days-old ones.
  • Work out whether Copilot or other AI is worth paying for, and roll it out safely.
  • Stay compliant (EU AI Act, GDPR) without becoming a lawyer.
  • Find out what grants, supports and training exist locally.
  • Look informed in front of the board, owners and staff.

Pains

  • Too much hype and vendor noise, and too little that applies to a 50–1,000-person firm in Munster.
  • Not sure what's already included in existing Microsoft licences.
  • Worried about data leaks and staff using public AI tools without guidance.
  • No in-house data or AI person, and hiring one is slow and expensive.
  • Past IT projects that ran long and delivered little.
  • No time to track AI news, rules and events.

Gains

  • One five-minute read a week with one idea you can try.
  • Worked examples on familiar tools (Excel, Teams, SharePoint, Power BI, SAP exports).
  • Plain-English summaries of rules, with links to the official sources.
  • A local calendar of AI events and supports worth attending.
  • A rough hours-saved estimate to take to the MD or finance director.
  • A trusted local person to ask when you're ready to run a pilot.

Pain relievers (what the newsletter does)

  • Filters the news down to 2–3 links that matter to Munster operations teams.
  • Includes a "Microsoft corner" with one relevant Copilot or Power Platform change, linked to Microsoft's release notes.
  • Has a "Try this" box with one safe prompt or workflow to test this week.
  • Links to official sources (EU AI Act Service Desk, DETE, DPC) rather than opinion.

Gain creators

  • A "time-back test" in every issue.
  • A free AI readiness checklist (lead magnet, aligned with System KR3 / SHA-31) and a one-page safe-AI guide (System KR5 / SHA-33).
  • Reply-to-ask: readers reply with a question and the best ones get answered in a later issue.

Target audience3

Primary personas

P1. "Operations Olivia": operations, finance or managing director

  • Works at a manufacturer, distributor or engineering firm in Cork, Kerry or Limerick with about 50–1,000 staff, running Microsoft 365 plus SAP/IFS or another ERP.
  • Owns the budget and the problem: overtime, slow month-end, quote backlogs.
  • Wants proof that AI saves hours, at low risk, without hiring.
  • Finds out about things through LinkedIn, the chamber, peers and local events.
  • What turns her into a client: a readiness assessment or pilot, then a €3–5k/month retainer.

P2. "IT Ian": IT manager or head of IT in the same firms

  • Gets asked about Copilot rollout, governance, data access and "shadow AI".
  • Wants clear Microsoft updates, security and compliance guidance, and practical rollout steps.
  • Finds out about things through Microsoft channels, Cork|Sec, Tech Industry Alliance and LinkedIn.
  • What turns him into a client: an internal champion who brings Process Power in for a prototype or forward-deployed engineering.

Secondary personas

S1. "Owner Orla": SME owner or professional-services partner (accountancy, legal, hospitality, especially Killarney and Kerry). She is curious but time-poor. She is a good source of referrals and smaller pilots, and is reachable through LEO, the chambers and the RDI Hub.

S2. Amplifiers: the support ecosystem. LEO business advisers, chamber staff, ENTIRE EDIH and RDI Hub teams, Enterprise Ireland development advisers, and meetup organisers. They forward useful content to their members and can offer speaking slots (Authority KR3).

S3. Local builders and future team. Power Platform and full-stack developers (Cork Devs, Future of Data Cork). They are potential subcontractors (Team KR3), readers and referrers.

Content pillars and format4

Pillars (rotate weekly)

  1. Time back (use cases): one operational AI workflow on M365 or ERP data, such as reconciliation, quotes, reports or document handling.
  2. Microsoft and stack: Copilot, Power Platform and Power BI changes that matter, linked to Microsoft's official release notes and roadmap.
  3. Safe and compliant: EU AI Act, GDPR and data-protection basics, AI policies and staff guidance, linked to official sources.
  4. Local supports and community: LEO, Enterprise Ireland, ENTIRE EDIH, the RDI Hub, research centres (Tyndall, Insight, CeADAR, IMaR), and events in Cork, Kerry and Limerick.
  5. Plan and lead: roadmaps, business cases and "how to brief your board".

Format (about a five-minute read, sent Tuesday mornings; test the send time)

  1. The big idea (300–500 words): one problem, one approach, the steps.
  2. Time-back test: how to estimate the hours saved in your own firm.
  3. This week in Irish AI: 2–3 curated links with one line each on why they matter.
  4. Microsoft corner: one relevant Copilot or Power Platform update.
  5. Try this: one safe prompt or workflow.
  6. Munster AI diary: upcoming events and supports.
  7. One CTA, rotating between reply, share, lead magnet and booking a call.

Repurposing: each issue becomes one LinkedIn post, one digital-clone video script (Authority KR2) and one carousel. The web archive lives on the newsletter site for SEO.

Rules: no invented stats or quotes. Anonymise client examples or label them as illustrative. Link to the primary source. Get written permission before naming any person or company.

Recommended platform8

Recommendation: Beehiiv. Start on the Free plan and move to Lite once the domain is live.

  • Beehiiv includes a hosted newsletter website and archive (good for SEO), subscribe forms and a Recommendation Network on Free (up to 2,500 subscribers, unlimited sends, per https://www.beehiiv.com/pricing, checked 17 Oct 2026).
  • According to the pricing comparison table, Lite adds a custom domain, automations (a welcome sequence and lead-magnet delivery), a referral programme, polls and surveys. That's from $49/month billed annually or $59/month billed monthly (USD, up to 1,000 subscribers). The page's feature list and comparison table disagree on whether custom domains are free, so confirm when you sign up.
  • Why not Substack: it's built around a creator brand, and its lead capture and automations are weaker for a B2B funnel. Kit (https://kit.com/) is a strong alternative if Shane prefers deeper automations and tagging over a hosted website and recommendations.
